## How many, never who

CrowdSense is the **sensing layer** of the Artefact Group event stack. It tells an organiser how many people are on site, where they are, how long they stay and where the queues are forming — second by second, from the first gate open to the last bus out.

It never knows *who*. Counts, not credentials. That is the line that separates CrowdSense from its sibling, [AdmitMe](https://github.com/AdmitMe-io), and it is a design rule, not a setting.

## What we build

<table>
<tr>
<td width="50%" valign="top">

### Vision
Presence, dwell, occupancy and queue analytics from any existing camera system, or from our own Optic Nodes. Nothing identifying leaves the edge.

</td>
<td width="50%" valign="top">

### Sense Nodes
Self-contained sensors that count without cameras. Battery, solar or mains. Drop them where the crowd is.

</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td width="50%" valign="top">

### Off-grid communications
A LoRa mesh that carries telemetry and messages across a site with no mobile coverage and no Wi-Fi. Built on [Meshtastic](https://meshtastic.org).

</td>
<td width="50%" valign="top">

### Pulse
The live-ops platform. Dashboards, alerting and forecasting that turn a sensor feed into a control room.

</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td width="50%" valign="top">

### CrowdSense Events
Core, Unit, Responder and Dispatch: crews, incidents, shifts and a control room that holds the night. Live at [core.crdsns.events](https://core.crdsns.events).

</td>
<td width="50%" valign="top">

### Muster and Travla
Crew welfare, check-in and safety on one link; team travel and trip planning on the other. Both run as installable PWAs over the same mesh.

</td>
</tr>
</table>
